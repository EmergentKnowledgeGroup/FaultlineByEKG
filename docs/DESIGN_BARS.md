# How it must look and read

The look is the product. People see a zoomed-out map and go quiet. Keep every one of these. Each is something a person notices on a real render, and a gate can pass while the picture is still wrong. So metrics are necessary and never sufficient: LOOK at the real render before calling anything done.

## Map
- One continuous happy path you can follow with your eye: left to right in rows, wrapping like a snake, aspect 1.3 to 2.2. Never a tall strip or a 14:1 ribbon.
- Follow any arrow from tail to head without losing it. No overlaps, no route through a box, no shared tracks, no route hidden behind a label, no diagonals, a clear straight run into every arrowhead.
- Return and retry routes run on the outside margins, never through the middle. Sinks and endings sit at corners or edges so incoming routes fan in from one side.
- Related boxes sit together. Branches of a decision fan out in different directions.
- Text stays in its own box. Never shrink type: cut words instead.
- Spread boxes out rather than cramming.
- Route labels are conditions in a few words, not sentences.

## Whole-system view
- Numbered stage blocks, each with a light box and a tinted halo in its own hue (the same hue as its dot in the nav and its box on the overview). Each stage has a screen-space pill, number and name, 13 px or more, at the top-left of its halo; it becomes the plain title in the halo's title band once the halo is large on screen. No name ever covers a box at a zoom where a box is readable.
- Hovering a stage pill (or a stage in the Regions list) lights its district and the roads in (cool) and out (warm); clicking a pill zooms to it. Nothing ever covers boxes. No floating tooltip over the whole map.
- The whole map opens showing only the map, the slim top bar and the dock: the nav, the details panel and the Regions list are closed (each toggle still works and is remembered for the session).
- Long roads (between stages) are soft ribbons: thin, faint and cool when zoomed out, a little brighter as you zoom in; the dock's "Long roads" button is on, faint or off. Lit, they are full strength, with their condition in screen-size type. The main path is off by default and, on, is one slim line of constant screen width with a soft glow. Roads of a lit loop or district keep a constant screen width too.
- Click a box anywhere: its roads light at full strength, every other road dims hard, and a person can follow one road across the whole map by eye.
- You can light up any stage or named loop while zoomed out, to show it to someone.
- Nothing is hidden because it's unglamorous: safety, failure and operations stages stay visible.
- No text under 11 screen pixels at any zoom. Below that, hide it and show dots, pills and glowing loops. Full text appears as you zoom in.
- Far zoom stays calm shapes: no handle chips, no names, stage pills and glowing halos. Pinning a stage or a loop zooms to it and opens the details panel; when its boxes still cannot be named on the map, the panel lists every one of them by name, each clickable. No tooltip over a box whose name is already on screen.
- An "Off by default" button tints the boxes that sit behind a switch (or that only a test reaches) so a reader sees where they are; everything else steps back.

## Never a box without its name (every chart except the whole map)
- At the default state on a 1920 x 1080 window, on EVERY chart, every box name and every route label reads at 11 screen pixels or more. Measured by script across all charts of a built page, never sampled. The overview poster is held to its box names only: its roads are listed in the panel when a stage is selected.
- How: "fit" never goes below the readable zoom. A chart that does not fit at that zoom with the side panels open gets the room by closing them for that chart only (the saved choice is untouched). If it still does not fit, it opens at the readable zoom, left and top aligned, and pans; the minimap shows where you are and a "Whole" button shows the whole shape small. Opening Regions, Evidence, Handles or the details panel never re-fits a chart under the line.
- A handle chip never stays on screen once its box has lost its name. A literal (a number, true, false, undefined, OK) is never a handle.

## Interaction
- Hover gives the same focus as click: routes light up, cool colour arrives, warm colour leaves, the rest fades. Contrast is high enough to read at a glance.
- Click pins. Arrow keys walk along routes. Double click zooms to a box. `/` searches. `Esc` clears. `0` fits.
- Every section leads into the next: where it came from, where it goes, typed hand-offs with a jump and a way back. A section never feels like an island.
- The left nav is grouped by stage: the start-here items, then one row per stage (number, colour dot, name, box count), collapsed; the current stage opens to list its parts by name. It scrolls only when the stages outgrow the window.
- Guides are compact.
- The box panel reads in this order: what it does, where it goes next, where it came from, the source link (on screen without scrolling at 1080p), then the rest. "Goes next" and "came from" are the real roads, on this chart and to other charts, together, the main path's own road first and marked. A box never says "nothing arrives here" above a list of arrivals.
- "From" and "Leads to" on a chart are real roads into and out of it, the main path's road first. The reading-order neighbour is shown apart as previous and next; it is never presented as a road.
- The main path is the declared list of boxes and only its CONSECUTIVE pairs light up. Every chart holding one of its boxes has the Main path toggle. "Walk the main path" steps through it (previous, next, "step n of N", the box's name), crossing charts on its own; the other declared paths walk the same way from their rows in the Regions list. "Tour the charts" walks the charts in reading order and says so.
- One self-contained file, no network.

## Words
- Plain English AND the code's own official names, always together. Plain words explain; official names locate. Scrubbing the code's vocabulary makes the owner lose track of where their own code engages.
- Never invent a name. If a term can't be translated without guessing, keep it and explain it.
- A route says the condition. A box says what happens and why it matters.
- Same concept, same words, everywhere: a thing the map draws is a "box", never a "step" (a Step is one kind of box).
- Text is never cut inside a number or a name. A sentence ends at a full stop followed by a space and a capital: 0.25 s, opts.trace, worker.py:32, e.g. and Dr. Smith do not end one, and nothing between two sentences is ever dropped.

## Evidence layer
- A switch, off by default. Off, the page is simply the atlas. On, outline says how we know and colour says what we found. Colour only where there is evidence.
- Drawn: normal, slightly muted. Suspected: dashed amber with "?". Seen: solid, with a count, routes thicken. Proved: double outline with a lock.
- Clicking a mark opens the docked panel: one sentence why, the evidence, what would make this a false alarm, the next step to strengthen it.
- Unproven never shows green. Anything not drawn yet is a list and a count beside the map, never empty space on it.

## Where it can break (the product's promise: its own page, in Start here, with a count)
- The questions the map's shape raises, in the order the data gives them: kept suspects, open candidates, then the waits with no timeout and the loops with no limit shown, grouped by stage. Where runs are loaded, the proved and seen rows lead.
- Each row: the question in plain words, the stage, the box (a click opens it on its chart), one line on why it matters. Filter by kind of check in plain words (a stop with no restart, one test reached from many places, handled but never sent, a flag never cleared, a wait with nothing to end it, a loop with no limit).
- Held-back leads sit in a closed section. Dismissed ones are reachable in a closed section with their reason: a dismissal is one reader's opinion.
- The top of the page says plainly: questions the map's shape raises, read and not run, not verdicts. It works with the evidence layer off.
- Endings and hand-offs to a person: no cut-off ("... and 17 more" is a defect), filter by outcome, grouped by stage. Off by default: the table lists only what is off or test-only, grouped by switch with what turns it on; the on-by-default boxes sit in a closed section.
- A total says what it counts, where it sits: "endings in the code" (the census) and "ending boxes" (the map) are not the same number.

## Two readers, one map
- A person and an agent point at the same stable box ids. The page offers a "copy pointer" for an agent; an agent's answers carry a link a person can open.

## Overview, the landing page
- A poster of the code: every stage's number and name reads at 13 px or more at the default fit on a 1920 x 1080 window, always (larger type for this chart, set when its geometry is built; the placer sizes boxes for it).
- Boxes carry their stage's colour, the same hue as the stage's dot in the nav and its halo on the whole map.
- Roads the overview does not draw are counted on the page and in the summary. Selecting a stage lists ALL its roads, drawn and not drawn, with counts, and offers a plain way in (open the stage).

## Part charts
- A stage opens on the part where the main path enters it, else the part with the most boxes, never a 3-box stray. In a stage's list, parts with one box or with no road inside sort last. A finished map shows its draft strip once (overview, whole map, scope guide), not on every chart; a stage part gives its room to the chart.
- A stage part has a breadcrumb: stage, part name, "2 of 6", previous and next. Hand-off chips name the part, and the stage only when it is a different stage. A small chart is never zoomed past about 110% at fit. Nothing that says nothing: no Regions section for one region, no sentence twice on screen, and a draft note says what is true ("nothing is waiting to be drawn", not "0 items").

## Scale (budgets, measured in a real browser, headless, software raster)
- First paint of the page under 3 s; opening the whole map under 2 s; every pan and zoom frame on the whole map under 50 ms.
- What makes it so: far level merges the roads into two paths; a stage's boxes, roads and labels are taken out of the document while its halo is off screen; box text and road labels exist only while the zoom could show them (every SVG text in the document is laid out again on every scale change, hidden or not); handle chips are laid out per stage, not for the whole map.

## Stillness, front doors and one word
- **The page stays still.** Previous chart and next chart live in the top bar, at one x and y on every chart, however the sidebar, the details panel or the chips below them change. Nothing opens or closes a panel by itself: the sidebar and the details panel change only when the person changes them (a chart that cannot fit with panels open opens at reading size and pans). After any chart change no hover applies until the mouse really moves, so a chip that slides under a still pointer lights nothing and no chart opens dimmed. A typed search stays on the chart it was typed on.
- **Zoom lands on something.** "Zoom to it" and "Zoom to where they read" aim at the pinned stage or loop (else the middle of the visible canvas), and the visible canvas excludes every open panel (the regions list, the handles panel, the legend, the docked card). The view is put where the most of the region's boxes sit, so at least three of its names read at 11 px or more. A pinned loop's roads are brighter than the roads around it and its label sits on the loop, above the member box nearest its middle.
- **One front door for failure.** "How it fails" is an entry under Start here on every map. It leads with one line (who hears), the counts by value sit once in the filter row under it, then it lists every failure and refusal grouped by who hears, filterable by who hears, by failure or refusal and by stage, none cut off. "None of these reach a person directly: the calling code decides" when none does. The overview's group boxes say who hears (counts), link to the page and list the whole group.
- **What can hold up the main path** is the first section of Where it can break (closed, so the page opens on the kept suspects): the declared path's own waits, gates and loops, in path order, each with its state (kept, open, held back, dismissed with its reason, or no question raised) and its limit or timeout. A dismissed box stays. The stuck advice points there first, then at machine waits elsewhere. A wait the model says is for a person is filed in its own closed group, outside the count of things to look at.
- **Walk a scenario** is a nav group under Start here: one row per declared path other than the main path. A click starts the walk; "Next stage »" skips to the first step in another stage; Esc leaves a walk or a tour; a road to the next step that is inferred (or not drawn) is said in the bar.
- **Trace path** is a toolbar control with a From and a To, each searching every chart. Ends on different charts open the whole map with the numbered list; a missing choice, the same box twice or no route each say so.
- **One word for people: "road".** An arrow is a road. The glance chart's lines are bundles (every road between the same two stages) and are called that. "N stages; the main path runs through K of them", never "from start to end". A box that is passed twice is one box and two steps: "8 steps, 7 different boxes (calls come back)".
- **Numbers defined once** (src/build/counts.mjs): the main path, a stage's roads (inside, arrive, leave), switches, failures by who hears, waits by what they wait for, the places on the main path that can hold it up. Every page prints these and says what they count.

## Room, tabs and the road a label belongs to
- **Room first.** The details panel starts closed (it is 340 px of the chart's room): a pin brings it, clearing the pin takes it away, and a choice made with the Details button is never undone. The fit's margins are small (a chart's own padding is already 20 units), the strip along the bottom is kept only when the zoom bar has no place along the bottom edge where it covers no box, and the header is one row shorter. Measured on every chart at 1920 x 1080: a box off the canvas is rare and only on the densest charts. A chart that still cannot fit opens at reading size and says so: a soft fade and a "more" tab sit on each cut edge, and the zoom bar sits where it covers no box.
- **A tab says something.** A handle fits on its tab whole (up to 30 characters on a box wide enough for it). One that does not: a start that three or more tabs of the chart share ("Parser.") is left out of them and said once in the chart's header; what is still too long is cut in the middle, so its start and its end both show; two tabs never read the same when their full names differ; the full name is a tooltip and is in the details panel. A handle held by three or more boxes and by 40% of a chart's tabs is said once in the header and drawn on none of them. A tab never sits on a box's name; a long tab gives way to a shorter cut of itself where it would sit on a road or a label. Under 10% of tabs are cut.
- **A label says which road is its own.** A label that sits beside its road has a dot on that road at the nearest point, its words against the road (left-aligned when the road is on the left, right-aligned when on the right) and its pill within 1.5 units of the road. The words of a label are within 8 px of their own road at the default view (median 5), and every label not on its road carries the dot. Nothing in the geometry moves, so the hard counts are what the placer measured.
- **A whole-chart view still says what is in it.** "All at once" is a smaller view: names are drawn down to 8.5 px and road conditions to 8 px, the zoom bar says "Smaller view", and Reading size is the way back. The 11 px rule stays for the default view and for every other zoom.
- **Names are not cut while there is room.** The overview poster names every stage and ending in full and wraps to more lines; the chart's own name in the breadcrumb wins room over the stage name, the counts and the handle chips; a part name in the sidebar has a tooltip with its whole name.
- **Small things.** No halo blob behind boxes you can read; a box that is off by default is dashed and paler but still reads; a box's card sits under the box it describes, never on it; the minimap goes to the corner where it covers fewer boxes; "1 box" and "1 road"; a one-box chart says it is one box.
- **Phone.** No sideways page scroll at 360 or 390 px wide on a chart, the overview, the whole map or a guide: the breadcrumb wraps and its buttons share a row.

## Laptops, the walk, the first screens
- **A laptop-sized window (under 1500 px wide) is a first-class view.** The sidebar starts as its slim rail (the person's own choice is kept), the Regions bar of a part chart sits in the toolbar and not over the chart's corner, and the chart is fitted by what is drawn (plus 3 units) with 4 px margins. Box names keep 11 px at the default view. Road conditions may be drawn as small as 9.4 px there (they are drawn at the same zoom as the names, so no chart is cut for them): this lowers the 11 px bar for road conditions on those windows and is a deliberate exception (`NARROW_LABEL` in viewer.js). At 1440 x 900 no chart of a map of several hundred boxes is cut at its default view; no box shows under half its width and no road condition is cut mid-word.
- **The Regions list never throws the chart away.** Opening it makes room only when the chart stays readable and within one zoom step; otherwise the list lies over the chart and the chart stays exactly where it was. The top of the chart never moves by 40 px or more.
- **A walk step shows its whole lit set**: the box, every box it lights, those roads and their labels, on the canvas, clear of the walk bar, the dock row and the details panel, at the smallest zoom that still reads names and never above 100%. The zoom of the step before is kept when the step fits at it.
- **A box that is off by default keeps its name at full strength**; one quiet mark says off (a dashed outline and a paler box). A lit or pinned box is never pale. The legend has an entry for it.
- **A lit forward road is solid** (cool in, warm out); only a return or retry road is dashed, as in the legend. The hover card avoids everything the pointer lit (box, road, label) or is not drawn.
- **A cut tab is plainly a cut**: the mark is three middle dots set apart in a dimmer colour, never an ellipsis (its low dots read as an underscore in the code font, and names are full of underscores).
- **The zoom bar** keeps its home (bottom-left) while nothing is under it. A chart that fills the canvas has something drawn there, so the bar stands at the first other corner where nothing is (the top-right, then the bottom-right, then the top-left: only the four corners, nudged along their edges by up to 60 px when covered, never the middle of an edge); it is chosen when the chart opens, and again when the canvas changes size or a panel over the right of the canvas opens or closes, and then it stays put. Only when no place is clear does the chart move to the top of its own slack and then shrink to leave a strip. The bar is opaque. A "more" tab never sits on a box, a road condition or a lit road. "All at once" says what it draws once, in the zoom bar, and puts no banner over the boxes.
- **Names are not cut beside room**: a stage pill on the whole map runs on to the next pill in its row and wraps to a second line before it is cut; flow bar chips share the room beside a short middle; the sidebar wraps a long stage name (up to three lines).
- **The poster**: every stage reads at full strength (a stage off the main path is not dimmed), an ending pill steps back (80%), and no stage cushion is drawn unless the stage is pointed at or pinned.
- **Where it can break opens on the questions**: one note (read from the map, not run: said once), one line of numbers, the filter (kinds with nothing in the open sections are behind one small disclosure), then the first kept suspect with its evidence open. One text width (88ch) and one link style on every guide page. **How it fails**: every description starts at its card's left edge, the counts by who-hears are in the filter once, the stage filter is drawn like the chips.

## Pins, the zoom bar, the whole map, tabs and the laptop's words
- **A pin brings its whole lit set onto the canvas, and clearing it gives the chart back.** The lit set is the box, the boxes its roads reach, those roads and their road conditions (and the handle tabs they carry). After a click on a box none of it is cut by the window or lies under the details panel, and the pinned box itself never is. A pin never zooms in; it pans as little as it can, zooms out only as far as it must, and goes below reading size (the same smaller view as "All at once", names down to 8.5 px, and under that calm shapes) only when the lit set can be shown no other way. Esc, a click on empty canvas, or any other way the pin goes puts the chart back within 8 px of where it was before the pin, with the same boxes on the canvas, unless the person has moved the view since. A panel that opens or closes on the right never moves the chart (it stays anchored at the left). No ring is drawn around the canvas after a click and Esc (a keyboard-focus ring is drawn only for a person who tabbed into it). **This lowers the 11 px bar for a pin whose lit set is too big for the canvas at reading size** (a deliberate exception: `pinReveal` in viewer.js; set `SMALL_NAME` equal to `MINPX` to forbid it).
- **The zoom bar has one place and one set of buttons.** Its place is one of the four corners, chosen once per chart (bottom left, or the first clear corner on a chart that fills the canvas), and it is the same in every state (a walk keeps it at home beside the walk bar, a pin, a click on +) except one: a panel that would cover it moves it. Handles, the Legend and the docked card lie over the right of the canvas, and the details panel takes the right edge; while one is open the bar stands in the first corner that is clear of the panel and of every box and tab (a covered corner may be nudged up to 60 px along its edges), so it is always visible and never behind a panel. On a chart too big to read whole, where no corner is clear with the panel open, the chart gives the bar a strip along the top and is cut a little more at the bottom, as the panel already cuts it at the side. The Legend does not refit the chart: with it open the bar is always visible, and on the busiest charts it may lie on a box. The bar goes back to its own place when the panel closes. Measured by script on every chart of a built page at 1440 x 900 with Handles and with Details open: hidden 0, on a box 0, on a tab 0. Apart from that a button is where the pointer left it, and three clicks on + all land. Its buttons are always Fit, All at once and 100% (All at once is dimmed on a chart that already shows whole at reading size; Fit keeps its name on a chart that cannot fit: its tooltip says what it does); the note about the smaller view lies beside the bar, not in it. At the default view nothing is under the bar: no box, tab, corner badge or road condition, and no road runs more than 30 px under it (`barSpot`, `fitTarget1`). A chart too big to show whole at reading size has its margins give way (down to 2 px) before a tab or a badge is cut.
- **100% on the whole map lands on a stage**: the one nearest the middle of what is visible, at actual size, its top left (and so its title) in view when it is bigger than the canvas.
- **The +N list in the flow bar lies wholly inside the window**, is as tall as the room under its chip, and scrolls, so every row can be reached.
- **The Evidence chip is on a chart opened from a link** at every window width (it was looked for inside the dock, and on a laptop-sized window the Regions bar sits in the toolbar).
- **On a laptop-sized window the top bar names "Where it can break" and "How it fails"** in words with their counts, where the how-to line was. The rail there is 46 px of pictures and numbers: a crack for where it can break, an arrow that stops at a wall for how it fails (not an X, which reads as close or error), and a name-and-count card at once on hover or focus; the menu button opens the whole list.
- **Long roads, "faint", at Fit is far fainter than a stage outline** (ribbon opacity .07 dark, .055 light at the far level); its states are the ones its tooltip names.
- **A tab is laid out where it will be drawn**: clear of the road condition at the position the pill is drawn at (labelShifts), at the growth the viewer gives it at the zoom the chart opens at, with a shorter cut when words would be under it, and the fit holds the tabs and corner badges at their drawn size (the fit used the laid-out size, so a tab at the edge of the chart was cut by the window). A tab with no clean place goes where it touches the fewest words (a road condition or the box's own name most, a road least).
- **No tab lies on another tab or on another box's name at the size the chart opens at.** What the layout could not keep clear at the growth the viewer gives the tab is cut shorter with the cut mark (its full name stays in its tooltip) until it clears, never below nine characters. Corner badges are laid out at the size they are drawn, and a place on a tab or a road condition counts against a badge more than a place on a road.
- **A road condition wraps in even lines.** A number stays with the word before it ("above 0", "not 1") and its unit stays with it ("within 2 s"); a last line is not one lone word when a split inside the same widest line avoids it.
- **Reading text shows no markup and no internal id.** Text in backticks is code, drawn in the code font; the id of a box a check wrote into a sentence is that box's name; "stage s_add" is the stage's name. The Where it can break, How it fails and Evidence pages and the details panel all go through the same two helpers.
- **The first screen says what it is**: one quiet line under the summary, on the overview only (what the page maps, that Faultline made it, a link to the project), and one line beside the census row saying what "Fully drawn / Accounted for" means (each counted item has a box or a stated reason, read from the code, not run).
- **The walk bar's stage chip is never cut to "1 · ..."**: it shows all of the stage's name, or without the path's name beside it, or the stage's number alone (the name stays in its tooltip).

## Reading words, box names, the bar with a panel open, the whole map's balance
- **Evidence is a plain sentence.** Each check writes its evidence as one to three sentences a person can read: no check word first ("candidate:", "decision", "lead:"), no road id (a road is told as "from A to B, when ..."), no box name said twice (a box id appears once, in the sentence, and the page turns it into the box's name), verbs that agree ("1 of them sits", "2 of them sit"), and code in backticks. The note to whoever checks a question (what a dismissal has to answer) stays on the evidence for the agent's commands and the agent file; the page's data leaves it out (`forReader` in `src/build/atlasdata.mjs`). A finding's key never changes with its wording.
- **A box's name inside a sentence is set apart in one way.** In a question, a reason or a line of evidence, the name sits on a soft neutral tint (`.bk-nm`, a tenth of the text colour behind the words) in the sentence's own font and colour, rounded and kept whole across a line break. It is not a link (no teal, no underline: links are `hlink`), not code (no code font, no outline), and it adds no character, so a copy of the sentence is the plain words. In Print the tint is kept and a little stronger. It works in the bold question line and in regular text, in both themes. A name used as a heading (a loop's title on a hold row) is not set apart: it is the heading.
- **A box name wraps like a road condition.** The same rule in `balanceLines` (`src/engine/layout.mjs`): the same words in the same order, the same number of lines, never wider than the greedy wrap, so a box keeps the size it was placed with; a last line is not one lone word when a split inside the same widest line avoids it; a number stays with its comparison word or its unit ("to / 40 characters" when that is the only way to avoid a lone word). The count line of an ending pill ("Success endings" over "(12)") is a count beside its name and stays alone. It applies to every chart and both posters, in `gridLayout` for new layouts and in `evenLabels` (`src/build/atlasdata.mjs`) for layouts placed before the rule.
- **The zoom bar steps aside for a panel and comes back.** See the zoom bar bullets above: four corners only, never the middle of an edge; the same place in every state except that a panel over it moves it for as long as the panel is open.
- **The whole map is centred by what is drawn.** At the default Fit the gap left of the leftmost halo or stage pill equals the gap right of the rightmost one (titles included), within a few pixels, at 1920 and at 1440 (`centreWhole` in `viewer/whole.js`; the map is centred in the room a left drawer and a right panel leave). Only the view moves.
- **"All at once" is drawn pressed only after a person pressed it.** A pin that has to be shown whole at a smaller size says so in the note beside the bar, but the button is not pressed; and that note never lies on a "more" tab (the tabs keep clear of it).
- **Halos and Labels live inside Regions** (the toolbar shows them once Regions is open) on a chart with several regions; the whole map has neither (its districts and stage pills are always drawn). Each changes the drawing where it can: Halos at a zoomed-out view (below about 55% on a stage chart, below about 30% on the poster), Labels cycling numbers, names, none at the far view (below about 30%) (compared by pixels in `viewer/layers.verify.mjs`). Where a button would change nothing (the boxes can be read, and no halo is drawn behind a box you can read) it is not left looking dead: it is dimmed with a dashed edge, says why in its tooltip and in one line at the top of the Regions list ("Halos and Labels show when the map is zoomed out. Zoom out to try them."), does nothing when pressed, and comes alive at the zoom where it works (`layerIdleSync` in `viewer/regions.js`).
- **A double-click means the box the first click landed on.** The first click pins the box, opens the details panel and refits the view, so by the second press the box can have moved from under the pointer. The page keeps the first click's box (`DBL` in `viewer/viewer.js`), claims the second press and click for it (no word is selected in the panel, no road is picked, no other box is pinned), and acts on that box: a stage on the poster opens its own chart; any other box (an ending group, a box on a chart) is zoomed to, as the help says. A single click is unchanged.
- **The phone.** On a narrow screen (up to 800 px) or a phone held sideways (short and under 1000 px wide), the poster and the whole map are not drawn: a plain list of the stages stands in their place (`viewer/phone.js`, `viewer/phone.css`), with a line that the full map reads best on a bigger screen, the two reading pages, and a tap on a stage opens its chart. The header is one short row. A chart of one stage keeps its drawing and its "zoom to where names read" card. Nothing is clipped by the screen edge and the page never scrolls sideways. A desktop window is not changed by any of it.
- **A held-back lead on the main path says what it caught.** On the "What can hold up the main path" rows, a held-back swallowed-error lead reads "An error is caught and not passed on", then the evidence sentence with the code's own comment quoted whole (up to 140 characters), and says it is only a lead: held back and not counted as a question. The bare question "This failure disappears here" is never shown there.
- **Where it can break says what it was read from.** The first note reads "Questions the map's shape raises, read from the code at commit X, not run. Nothing was executed, so some of these questions will be wrong. None is a verdict." The commit is the page's pin (`META.pin` or `META.source.pin`, shortened to seven characters; a source with no commit says "in source tree" instead); a page with no pin says "read from the code and the map" and leaves it out. The word is "question", never "bug".
- **One name for a check.** The evidence panel's tag for a finding uses the words of the Where it can break page ("A wake-up that can be missed"), not the check's key.
- **Small things.** A tab's cut lands on a word or path boundary at both ends when one is within reach ("Open /" ... "orders", not "Open /" ... "rders"); the first open follows the operating system's light or dark setting until the person has chosen; a #reading link opened directly keeps "Tour the charts" in the top bar; in light mode the handle tabs in a header row match the tabs on the chart and START and END on the poster are white on a deep green; a section's one-line take may run to two lines instead of being cut mid-word; the Regions list on a short window (the poster at 1440 x 900) shows all its stages.

## The tool-map page (a page made by `faultline map`)

A page whose data says `meta.toolMap` keeps every bar above except where this section says otherwise. It is made by programs, and the page must never read as if a person or a model wrote it. Everything here is switched on by `meta.toolMap` alone: an agent-made page looks and reads as it always did (`test/viewer.verify.mjs --only toolmap` holds both).

**Where it came from**
- Every chart says "READ BY A PROGRAM". The first line says a program read the code and drew this and no agent wrote any of it. When an agent wrote words, the page says "Words by" and the model. Nothing on it says "proven", "verified" or "complete".
- It opens on the Reader: what the code is (the project's own words with their source, or a plain statement that none was found; never a purpose the page made up), what was read, what was not read, what is open. Every number on it comes from the reading's one numbers record. The page counts nothing itself.
- "Read from the code, not run" is said once at the top of Where it can break and wherever a question is shown. The word is "candidate" or "question", never "bug".

**What the tool is sure of and what it flags**
- A box or road the tool is not confident in is drawn dashed with a "?". Its Details panel opens with "Not confident." and the reasons in sentences, not codes. A road the tool inferred says "Lower trust" and why. The first screen says how many of each there are.
- A person or an agent can research those and only those. The page says so in one line and never invites a reader to change a confident claim.
- Where the code does not settle something, the page says "not known from the code" (who hears about a failure, what a wait waits for). It never fills the gap and never uses the hand-written map's "not said yet".

**Stages, charts and what is not drawn**
- Stages are groups of directories, named for the folder or file that holds most of them, largest first. Every list of them says it is largest first and does not claim a run order. The page never says "in story order", "the story runs", or "from start to end", The numbers on the stages are their place in that list, not a run order.
- There is no whole-system map and no Regions list of a whole. The Regions button reads "Stages and loops" and the search button reads "Search all charts". No link points at a `whole` chart.
- The overview is a poster of the stages and the groups of endings (success, failure, refusal, neutral). A double click on a stage lists its functions in a panel, since there is no stage chart to open; a click on a function opens its function chart. One chart is drawn for each function worth a picture, at most 21 boxes to a chart (a bigger one is cut into parts), with a card for each function it calls. The 11 px rule holds on every one of them.
- A function with no chart of its own is read as an outline in the Reader, with who calls it and which journeys pass through it. A page stays under 16 MB, so on a large codebase the functions on a journey are drawn first, then the ones with the most boxes.
- The top bar names two pages, "How it fails" and "Where it can break", each with its count. They are words in the top bar at 1024, 1366 and 1440 px wide and leave the top bar at 1920 px (measured on a built page).

**Journeys and endings (the Reader)**
- A journey is a run from an outside trigger. It is listed in a short form (about 15 to 25 steps) and in full, and it can be walked step by step across charts. A call the tool could not follow, a choice it could not make and a break are each shown as such.
- A journey that reaches its last line reads "<name> finishes", or "Returns to <caller>" when something calls it. "Stopped here, no ending reached" is kept for a real break or raise, and says why. Every journey finishes one of these ways.
- "No main path found" is said when no example in the project's README or docs names an entry and no journey clearly leads. The rule that chose the main path is printed on the page.

**The phone (up to 800 px wide, or a phone held sideways)**
- The poster and the whole map are not drawn. A plain list of the stages stands in their place, says it is largest first and not run in order, and says the full map reads best on a bigger screen. A tap on a stage lists its functions; a tap on a function opens its chart. No sideways scroll at 390 px (measured).

**Known limits**
- The poster has a floor on its zoom. On the page of a public example with 24 stages it opened at 37% on a 1366 x 768 window and at 44% on 1920 x 1080 (measured). Whether a poster fits whole on a smaller window depends on how many stages it has.
- A page for code with many hundreds of functions has outlines where it has no charts. That is the 16 MB limit working.
